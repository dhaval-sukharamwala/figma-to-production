The product decision underneath

        
This isn't a build tool. It's a decision about where consistency should live.

      

    

    

      

        
Business goal

        
Cut the gap between what design approves and what customers actually see — across three platforms, without adding engineering headcount.

      

      

        
The decision

        
Move consistency out of documentation and memory, and into the build. If a rule can be enforced by a machine, it shouldn't depend on a person remembering it.

      

      

        
Outcome

        
    
          
  • Approved changes reach production in one review, not one sweep per platform
    • 
          
  • Visual drift between platforms stops accumulating
    • 
          
  • Dark mode becomes a value swap, not a rewrite
    • 
          
  • Contrast is checked at build time, not remembered
    • 
          
  • Engineers spend the time on features instead of hex codes
    • 
        

      

    

  







  

    
what just happened

    
No engineer touched a UI file.

    
The pipeline removes manual retyping, not deployment. Web picks up the change on the next deploy; mobile picks it up on the next app release.

    
That's the idea behind this demo. Design tokens become a shared source of truth, allowing one change in Figma to flow consistently across web and mobile without engineers updating individual UI files. It's the kind of product infrastructure I enjoy designing—where systems, tooling, and user experience work together instead of drifting apart.
